2. How did Actual compare to the Expected plan?

  • 101: same result, different routing. The plan had Inventory hand off to Accounting; instead the Boss called Accounting itself and coordinated. The customer draft was more careful than planned: it promised no arrival date.
  • 102: matched Boss → Facilities → Accounting almost exactly. Extras: an optional landlord draft and the Boss re-checking cash.
  • 103: matched the sequential plan: Bulldog could ship after #501 was paid, the PO was drafted, no payment was requested, and the ticket stayed blocked on cash. Extras: a second vendor lookup and a third price test.
  • Pattern: differences were extra read-only calls and Boss-centred routing, never wrong numbers or unsafe actions.

3. What would have been simpler as one agent with tools?

  • Ticket 102 is the clearest case. One operations agent could check the lease, payment history and cash, queue the $2,400 rent, and update the ticket. With no judgment calls, the relay only added hand-offs and repeated context.
  • Tickets 101 and 103 would also need less back-and-forth with one coordinating agent holding every tool, because one context keeps all the facts.
  • Why keep a team? Narrow tool allowlists act as guardrails (only Accounting can queue payments), which matters as the shop grows. Either way, human approval stays required.

4. Three new problems this team and its tools can already solve

  1. Crest Mug stock-out (0 on hand). Inventory runs check_stock and picks Elm City Gifts (3-day lead time, no open invoices). Accounting drafts a PO at the $3.50 unit cost and queues it if cash allows. Customer Service drafts the reply.
  2. Next month's rent (lease #1, due 2026-10-02). Facilities runs get_lease_status and list_payments to avoid paying twice. Accounting queues $2,400, and get_cash_balance flags the rent early so other spending can't use it first.
  3. Bulk discount or duplicate-bill check, e.g. caps (40 on hand, $6 cost, $22 list). check_price_margin keeps offers above cost, and request_payment refuses wrong amounts and duplicates. The Boss sets the offer, and Customer Service drafts it.
